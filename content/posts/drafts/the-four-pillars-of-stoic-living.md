Summary: A brief exploration of the four cardinal virtues of Stoicism and how they anchor a life of purpose.

Stoicism is often misunderstood as the repression of emotion. In truth, it is the mastery of judgment, aimed at living in accordance with nature. At the heart of this practice lie four cardinal virtues: Wisdom, Courage, Justice, and Temperance.

Wisdom is the ability to navigate the complex world around us with clarity. It involves discerning what is within our control and what is not. When we focus our energy on our own choices rather than external outcomes, we cultivate a mind that is resilient.

Courage is not merely the absence of fear, but the willingness to act rightly despite it. It is the strength to stand by our principles when it is easier to compromise. For me, this often looks like staying true to my development path despite the challenges of building in a constrained environment.

Justice is the virtue of community. It reminds us that we are part of a larger whole and that our actions impact others. Treating people with fairness and kindness, regardless of their status, is essential to a meaningful life.

Temperance is the virtue of moderation and self-control. In a world of excess, the ability to say "enough" is a superpower. By restraining our impulses, we gain the freedom to focus on what truly matters.

These virtues are not destinations, but daily practices. I aim to weave them into my work as a developer and my life in general. They provide a stable foundation in an uncertain world.
